Auto-Balanced Bridge (ABB) MethodTheoretical Foundations & Mathematical DerivationsThe Auto-Balanced Bridge (ABB) architecture represents the primary instrumentation standard for high-precision impedance measurements from low frequencies up to approximately $120\,\text{MHz}$. The working mechanism relies on maintaining a virtual ground potential at the low terminal of the Device Under Test (DUT), thereby neutralizing the effect of parasitic impedances to ground and isolating the complex impedance vector of the film capacitor.The complex impedance of a real metallized film capacitor is modeled by the series equivalent circuit:$$Z_x = R + j\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)$$In the ABB configuration, a known AC voltage stimulus $V_x$ is applied to the High-Force ($H_{\text{cur}}$) terminal of the DUT. The resulting current $I_x$ flows through the capacitor to the Low-Force ($L_{\text{cur}}$) terminal. An active Operational Transimpedance Amplifier (OTA) detects any current flow at the $L_{\text{cur}}$ node and dynamically adjusts its output voltage $V_r$ through a known range-feedback resistor $R_r$ to force the potential at the $L_{\text{cur}}$ terminal to exactly zero volts. Under these conditions, the nodal current balancing equation at the virtual ground is expressed as:$$I_x + I_r = 0 \implies \frac{V_x}{Z_x} + \frac{V_r}{R_r} = 0$$Solving for the complex impedance vector $Z_x$ yields:$$Z_x = -R_r \cdot \frac{V_x}{V_r}$$To extract the physical parameters from the raw measured complex ratio, the instrument decomposes the voltage ratio into real and imaginary parts:$$\frac{V_x}{V_r} = A + jB$$Substituting this ratio back into the impedance equation yields:$$Z_x = -R_r(A + jB) = R_s + jX_s$$Where the apparent series resistance $R_s$ and apparent series reactance $X_s$ are isolated as:$$R_s = -R_r A$$$$X_s = -R_r B$$At a single test frequency $\omega$, a conventional instrument reports the apparent series capacitance $C_s$ based on a simplified two-element capacitive model :$$C_s = -\frac{1}{\omega X_s} = \frac{1}{\omega R_r B}$$However, the true physical reactance of the film capacitor includes both the dielectric capacitance $C$ and the nanohenry-range equivalent series inductance $ESL$ ($L$). Near or above the self-resonant frequency (SRF), the inductive reactance counteracts the capacitive reactance, leading to a systematic overestimation of the apparent capacitance. To isolate the true frequency-independent capacitance $C$ and the nanohenry $ESL$, the instrument must perform measurements at two distinct frequencies, $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$, within the capacitive regime :$$X_{s1} = \omega_1 L - \frac{1}{\omega_1 C}$$$$X_{s2} = \omega_2 L - \frac{1}{\omega_2 C}$$Multiplying both equations by their respective angular frequencies yields:$$\omega_1 X_{s1} = \omega_1^2 L - \frac{1}{C}$$$$\omega_2 X_{s2} = \omega_2^2 L - \frac{1}{C}$$Subtracting the first equation from the second eliminates the capacitive term, allowing the direct isolation of the true equivalent series inductance $L$:$$L = \frac{\omega_1 X_{s1} - \omega_2 X_{s2}}{\omega_1^2 - \omega_2^2}$$To isolate the true capacitance $C$, we multiply the equations to establish a common inductive multiplier:$$\omega_1 \omega_2^2 X_{s1} = \omega_1^2 \omega_2^2 L - \frac{\omega_2^2}{C}$$$$\omega_1^2 \omega_2 X_{s2} = \omega_1^2 \omega_2^2 L - \frac{\omega_1^2}{C}$$Subtracting the second equation from the first and solving for $C$ provides the exact parameter extrapolation formula:$$C = \frac{\omega_1^2 - \omega_2^2}{\omega_1 \omega_2^2 X_{s1} - \omega_1^2 \omega_2 X_{s2}}$$Comprehensive Solved Numerical ExampleThe baseline DUT possesses the following true physical parameters:Intrinsic Dielectric Capacitance ($C$) = $2.919\,\mu\text{F} = 2.919 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F}$Intrinsic Equivalent Series Inductance ($L$) = $20.90\,\text{nH} = 2.090 \times 10^{-8}\,\text{H}$Intrinsic Equivalent Series Resistance ($R$) = $15.01\,\text{m}\Omega = 15.01 \times 10^{-3}\,\Omega$The instrument performs a sweep and evaluates the DUT at two discrete, highly demanding test points:Test Point 1: $f_1 = 100\,\text{kHz} \implies \omega_1 = 2\pi \times 10^5 \approx 628,318.5307\,\text{rad/s}$Test Point 2: $f_2 = 300\,\text{kHz} \implies \omega_2 = 2\pi \times 3 \times 10^5 \approx 1,884,955.5922\,\text{rad/s}$At $\omega_1$:Capacitive Reactance: $X_{C1} = -\frac{1}{\omega_1 C} = -\frac{1}{628,318.5307 \times 2.919 \times 10^{-6}} \approx -0.5452372\,\Omega$Inductive Reactance: $X_{L1} = \omega_1 L = 628,318.5307 \times 2.090 \times 10^{-8} \approx 0.0131319\,\Omega$Net Apparent Reactance: $X_{s1} = 0.0131319 - Voltage Surge Discharge MethodTheoretical Foundations & Mathematical DerivationsThe Voltage Surge Discharge Method is an active time-domain characterization technique used to isolate equivalent series inductance ($ESL$) and resistance ($ESR$) by measuring the underdamped ringing current waveform generated when a pre-charged capacitor is discharged through a low-impedance switch. The physical discharge loop is modeled as a series $RLC$ circuit:      +-----+      +-----+      +-----+
+-----|  C  |------|  L  |------|  R  |-----+
|     +-----+      +-----+      +-----+     |
|                                           |
+---------------------------------+
The second-order linear differential equation governing the transient loop current $i(t)$ is:$$L \frac{d^2 i(t)}{dt^2} + R \frac{di(t)}{dt} + \frac{1}{C} i(t) = 0$$Dividing by the loop inductance $L$ yields the standard form:$$\frac{d^2 i(t)}{dt^2} + \frac{R}{L} \frac{di(t)}{dt} + \frac{1}{LC} i(t) = 0$$The roots of the characteristic equation in the Laplace domain are :$$s^2 + \frac{R}{L}s + \frac{1}{LC} = 0 \implies s_{1,2} = -\alpha \pm \sqrt{\alpha^2 - \omega_0^2}$$Where the Neper frequency $\alpha$ and the undamped resonant angular frequency $\omega_0$ are defined as:$$\alpha = \frac{R}{2L}$$$$\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$$For the underdamped condition ($\alpha < \omega_0$), the roots are complex conjugates, $s_{1,2} = -\alpha \pm j\omega_d$, where the damped resonant angular frequency $\omega_d$ is :$$\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \alpha^2} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{LC} - \left(\frac{R}{2L}\right)^2}$$Using the initial conditions of the fully charged capacitor, where $q(0) = Q_0 = C V_0$ and the initial current $i(0) = 0$, the time-dependent current waveform is solved as :$$i(t) = \frac{V_0}{\omega_d L} e^{-\alpha t} \sin(\omega_d t)$$To extract the physical parameters from the experimental waveform, the period of the damped oscillation $T_d$ is measured from the time interval between the first and third zero-crossings :$$T_d = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_d} \implies \omega_d = \frac{2\pi}{T_d}$$The rate of decay is quantified by the logarithmic decrement $\delta$, calculated from two consecutive positive current peaks, $I_1$ and $I_2$, separated by a full period $T_d$:$$\delta = \ln\left(\frac{I_1}{I_2}\right) = \alpha T_d \implies \alpha = \frac{\delta}{T_d}$$Once $\alpha$ and $\omega_d$ are determined, the undamped resonant frequency is computed:$$\omega_0 = \sqrt{\omega_d^2 + \alpha^2}$$Using the nominal capacitance $C$ (which must be independently characterized at a low reference frequency), the total loop inductance $L_{\text{loop}}$ and series resistance $R_{\text{loop}}$ are calculated as:$$L_{\text{loop}} = \frac{1}{\omega_0^2 C}$$$$R_{\text{loop}} = 2 \alpha L_{\text{loop}}$$To isolate the true capacitor $ESL$ ($L$) and $ESR$ ($R$), the calibrated external parasitic inductance ($L_{\text{ext}}$) and resistance ($R_{\text{ext}}$) of the fixture must be subtracted :$$L = L_{\text{loop}} - Assuming a symmetric system where the source and load ports are terminated in $Z_0 = 50\,\Omega$, the transmission coefficient $S_{21}$ is defined as the ratio of the transmitted voltage wave at Port 2 to the incident voltage wave at Port 1 :$$S_{21} = \frac{2 Z_{\text{DUT}}}{2 Z_{\text{DUT}} + Z_0}$$Solving for $Z_{\text{DUT}}$:$$2 Z_{\text{DUT}} \cdot (1 - S_{21}) = Z_0 \cdot S_{21} \implies Z_{\text{DUT}} = \frac{Z_0}{2} \cdot \frac{S_{21}}{1 - S_{21}}$$For impedances much smaller than the characteristic impedance ($Z_{\text{DUT}} \ll 25\,\Omega$, which corresponds to $S_{21} \ll 1$), the denominator approaches unity, yielding the linear approximation :$$Z_{\text{DUT}} \approx \frac{Z_0}{2} \cdot S_{21} = 25 \cdot S_{21}$$Substituting the real and imaginary parts of the complex transmission coefficient, $S_{21} = \text{Re}(S_{21}) + j\text{Im}(S_{21})$, into the exact formula:$$Z_{\text{DUT}} = 25 \cdot \frac{\text{Re}(S_{21}) + j\text{Im}(S_{21})}{(1 - \text{Re}(S_{21})) - j\text{Im}(S_{21})}$$Multiplying the numerator and denominator by the complex conjugate of the denominator, $(1 - \text{Re}(S_{21})) + j\text{Im}(S_{21})$:$$Z_{\text{DUT}} = 25 \cdot \frac{ + j\text{Im}(S_{21})}{(1 - When the wavefront encounters the shunt-connected capacitor DUT, the impedance discontinuity causes a portion of the wave to reflect back toward the generator.Because the rise time of the TDR step is extremely fast (typically $<35\,\text{ps}$), the high-frequency components of the step wavefront see the capacitive element of the DUT as a short circuit to ground, meaning the transient reflection is dominated by the series inductance ($ESL$) and resistance ($ESR$).The complex impedance in the Laplace $s$-domain is:$$Z_{\text{DUT}}(s) = R + sL$$The reflection coefficient in the Laplace domain is:$$\Gamma(s) = \frac{Z_{\text{DUT}}(s) - Z_0}{Z_{\text{DUT}}(s) + Z_0} = \frac{sL + R - Z_0}{sL + R + Z_0}$$Assuming the series resistance $R$ is much smaller than $Z_0$ ($R \ll Z_0$):$$\Gamma(s) \approx \frac{sL - Z_0}{sL + Z_0} = 1 - \frac{2 Z_0}{sL + Z_0} = 1 - \frac{2}{s + \frac{Z_0}{L}}$$Applying a step voltage input $V_{\text{inc}}(t) = V_0 \cdot u(t)$ (where $u(t)$ is the unit step function), the Laplace transform of the incident wave is $V_{\text{inc}}(s) = \frac{V_0}{s}$. The reflected voltage wave in the Laplace domain is:$$V_{\text{refl}}(s) = V_{\text{inc}}(s) \cdot \Gamma(s) = \frac{V_0}{s} \cdot \left( 1 - \frac{2}{s + \frac{Z_0}{L}} \right) = \frac{V_0}{s} - \frac{2 V_0}{s\left(s + \frac{Z_0}{L}\right)}$$Decomposing into partial fractions:$$\frac{1}{s\left(s + \frac{Z_0}{L}\right)} = \frac{L}{Z_0} \cdot \left( \frac{1}{s} - \frac{1}{s + \frac{Z_0}{L}} \right)$$$$V_{\text{refl}}(s) = \frac{V_0}{s} - \frac{2 V_0 L}{Z_0} \cdot \left( \frac{1}{s} - \frac{1}{s + \frac{Z_0}{L}} \right) = V_0 \left( \frac{1 - \frac{2 L}{Z_0}}{s} + \frac{\frac{2 L}{Z_0}}{s + \frac{Z_0}{L}} \right)$$Taking the inverse Laplace transform:$$v_{\text{refl}}(t) = V_0 \left( 2 e^{-\frac{Z_0}{L} t} - 1 \right) \cdot u(t)$$The total measured voltage wave on the oscilloscope is the sum of the incident and reflected waves:$$v_{\text{meas}}(t) = v_{\text{inc}}(t) + v_{\text{refl}}(t) = V_0 u(t) + V_0 \left( 2 e^{-\frac{Z_0}{L} t} - 1 \right) \cdot u(t) = 2 V_0 e^{-\frac{Z_0}{L} t} \cdot u(t)$$The transient waveform decays exponentially with a time constant $\tau$ governed by the ratio of the series inductance to the characteristic impedance:$$\tau = \frac{L}{Z_0}$$This exponential decay is measured by the oscilloscope, and the inductance is isolated:$$L = Z_0 \cdot \tau$$For non-ideal step excitation waveforms (finite rise time $t_r > 0$), the transient waveform is a convolution of the step rise time with the RLC decay envelope. To resolve this, we integrate the reflection coefficient waveform $\rho(t)$ over the duration of the transition to isolate the inductance :$$L = 2 Z_0 \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \rho(t) \, dt$$Where $t_1$ and $t_2$ correspond to the start and end of the inductive reflection peak.Comprehensive Solved Numerical ExampleThe true parameters of the DUT are:$C = 2.919\,\mu\text{F} = 2.919 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F}$$L = 20.90\,\text{nH} = 2.090 \times 10^{-8}\,\text{H}$$R = 15.01\,\text{m}\Omega = 15.01 \times 10^{-3}\,\Omega$The TDR system uses a $Z_0 = 50\,\Omega$ coaxial path and applies an ideal step voltage $V_0 = 1.0\,\text{V}$.Calculating the physical time constant $\tau$:$$\tau = \frac{L}{Z_0} = \frac{20.90 \times 10^{-9}}{50} = 4.180 \times 10^{-10}\,\text{s} = 418.0\,\text{ps}$$The total measured transient voltage waveform is:$$v_{\text{meas}}(t) = 2.0 \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{4.180 \times 10^{-10}}}\,\text{V}$$During physical testing, the high-bandwidth sampling oscilloscope records the transient waveform.
