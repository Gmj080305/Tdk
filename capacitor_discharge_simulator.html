RLC Discharge Model

The discharge loop is modeled as a source-free lumped-element series RLC network. After switch closure, the governing second-order ODE is:

Series RLC Discharge ODE Ltotal · d²i/dt² + Rtotal · di/dt + (1/CDUT) · i(t) = 0

where the total parasitic sums include device-specific contributions:

Ltotal = LESL + Lswitch + Ltrace
Rtotal = RESR + Rswitch + Rload

This is solved by the simulator using 4th-order Runge-Kutta integration of the equivalent state-space form:

dVC/dt = −i / CDUT
dIL/dt = (VC − R·IL) / L

Initial conditions: VC(0) = V₀, IL(0) = 0. The stored energy in the capacitor at t=0 is ½C·V₀², which discharges through the resistive and inductive elements.

Damping Regimes

The characteristic equation roots determine the circuit's transient response. Define:

α = Rtotal / (2Ltotal)  [Np/s, damping coefficient]
ω₀ = 1 / √(Ltotal · CDUT)  [rad/s, natural frequency]
RegimeConditionCurrent Response i(t)
Overdamped α > ω₀ i(t) = V₀/(2β·L) · [e−(α−β)t − e−(α+β)t]
β = √(α²−ω₀²)
Underdamped α < ω₀ i(t) = V₀/(ωd·L) · e−αt · sin(ωd·t)
ωd = √(ω₀²−α²)
Critically-Damped α = ω₀ i(t) = (V₀/L) · t · e−αt

The quality factor Q = ω₀/(2α) = (1/R)·√(L/C) quantifies oscillatory behavior. For Q > 0.5 (underdamped), ringing occurs. Precision capacitor testing typically targets overdamped or critically damped conditions to produce clean exponential waveforms for ESR/ESL extraction.

Switch Parasitic Physics

MOSFET Output Capacitance (Coss)

MOSFET output capacitance Coss = Cds + Cgd stores energy during the charge phase. At switch closure this energy:

Eparasitic = ½ · Coss · V²

injects into the load, distorting waveforms when CDUT < 1nF (comparable parasitic energy). The Miller plateau extends turn-on transition:

tplateau = Qgd / Igate_driver

GaN Common-Source Inductance Feedback

Any inductance Lcs shared between the power loop and gate loop generates negative feedback voltage during switching:

Vfeedback = −Lcs · dID/dt

With dI/dt > 100A/ns, even 0.5nH causes catastrophic gate interference. Kelvin source packaging (4-terminal TO-247-4L) isolates the gate return from the power path.

SCR Current Spreading

In a thyristor, conduction initiates adjacent to the gate electrode and spreads laterally at a finite velocity (~50–100µm/µs). The local current density before spreading:

Jlocal = Itotal / Aconducting(t)

exceeds the safe operating area for 1–5µs if dI/dt is unconstrained, causing localized thermal runaway. This spreading delay is the primary waveform distortion mechanism in SCR circuits.

Kelvin Sensing & 4-Terminal Packages

In a standard 3-terminal package, the high-current discharge path and gate-drive return share the common-source pin inductance Lcs. The gate-source voltage "seen" by the device is corrupted:

VGS_effective = VGS_applied − Lcs · dID/dt

4-terminal Kelvin packages (TO-247-4L, TOLL, DFN8×8) provide a dedicated Kelvin source pin bonded directly to the die without passing through the high-current bond wire stack. This completely decouples the gate-drive loop from the power loop, restoring the device's full physical switching speed.

Kelvin connection advantage in rise time:

Δtrise ≈ Lcs · Ipeak / Vgate_drive

For Lcs=2nH, Ipeak=50A, VGS=15V: Δtrise ≈ 6.7ns additional delay — fully eliminated with Kelvin packaging.

High-Frequency Probe Loading

A standard 10× passive probe presents Rprobe=10MΩ, Cprobe=10–15pF. At high frequencies, the capacitive reactance drops:

XC = 1 / (2π·f·Cprobe)

At 500MHz with C=10pF: XC=31.8Ω — severely loading high-impedance nodes, dampening HF components and distorting transition edges. The ground lead inductance (~1nH/mm) creates a resonant circuit:

fresonance = 1 / (2π·√(Lground·Cprobe))

For a 165mm (6.5in) ground clip: Lground≈190nH, fres≈100MHz — causing ringing artifacts on any edge with sub-10ns rise time. Solutions:

Coaxial Impedance Matching

For sub-nanosecond rise times, interconnects become distributed transmission lines. Any impedance discontinuity causes reflections characterized by:

Γ = (ZL − Z₀) / (ZL + Z₀)  [Reflection coefficient]
VSWR = (1 + |Γ|) / (1 − |Γ|)

For zero reflections: Γ=0, VSWR=1, requiring matched Z₀=50Ω throughout the signal path. A single impedance mismatch generates a reflected pulse with delay Δt = 2·Lcable/vp, where vp≈2×10⁸ m/s in coaxial cable.

For high-voltage pulsed loads, standard thin-film resistors introduce parasitic series inductance (~1nH) and fail thermally. Optimal termination uses exponentially tapered coaxial terminations with CuSO₄ liquid or tubular bulk-ceramic resistive elements, maintaining Z=50Ω continuously along the physical taper length.

GaN HEMT: 2DEG Channel Physics

GaN HEMTs use a two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) formed at the AlGaN/GaN heterojunction. Unlike silicon MOSFETs, there is no body diode and no minority carrier storage. Gate charge is ~50× lower than silicon, enabling sub-nanosecond transitions. The 2DEG electron density:

ns = (εAlGaN/q·dAlGaN) · (Vth − VGS)

The common-source inductance feedback (Vfeedback=−Lcs·dI/dt) is particularly destructive in GaN due to the extremely fast dI/dt (>100A/ns achievable). With Lcs=0.5nH and dI/dt=50A/ns: Vfeedback=−25V — exceeding VGS_max and causing gate oxide rupture. This is why Kelvin packaging is mandatory, not optional, for GaN HEMTs.

IGBT: Minority Carrier Tail Current

The IGBT's p-n-p bipolar drift region injects minority carriers (holes) during conduction. On turn-off, the MOS gate stops electron injection, but holes already stored in the drift region recombine at a rate governed by the minority carrier lifetime τhl:

Itail(t) = Itail0 · e−t/τhl

This tail current (typically 10–30% of IC, lasting 1–5µs) causes substantial conduction loss during turn-off and distorts the discharge waveform tail. Modern devices use proton irradiation or electron irradiation to reduce τhl, trading off forward voltage drop VCE(sat) against switching speed.

Inductive voltage overshoot during hard turn-off:

Vovershoot = Lstray · dIC/dt

For Lstray=20nH, dIC/dt=1A/µs: Vovershoot=20V above bus — requiring soft-shutdown (controlled gate ramp) to limit dI/dt and prevent IGBT overstress.
