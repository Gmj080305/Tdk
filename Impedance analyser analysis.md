Diagnostic Analysis of Impedance Spectroscopy Anomalies in Film Capacitor Qualification Using the Keysight E4990AAnalytical and Mathematical Diagnostics of Single-Frequency Parameter ExtractionMathematical Derivation of Single-Frequency Parameter ExtractionWhen evaluating physical passive components, the standard approach is to model the device under test ($\text{DUT}$) as a lumped element network. For a metallized film capacitor, the physical component is represented as a three-element series network comprising its intrinsic capacitance ($C$), its equivalent series inductance ($\text{ESL}$, denoted as $L$), and its equivalent series resistance ($\text{ESR}$, denoted as $R$). The complex impedance ($Z$) of this series $R-L-C$ network as a function of the angular frequency ($\omega = 2\pi f$) is expressed mathematically as :$$Z = R + j\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)$$Impedance analyzers such as the Keysight E4990A utilize the auto-balancing bridge ($\text{ABB}$) technique to measure the complex voltage and current vectors across the terminal interface. The instrument registers the polar parameters of magnitude ($|Z|$) and phase angle ($\theta$). Under standard single-frequency spot measurement configurations (such as $C_s$ or $L_s$), the instrument's firmware translates this measured vector into a simplified, two-element series model ($R_s-C_s$ or $R_s-L_s$).If the instrument is configured to extract series capacitance ($C_s$), it mathematically assumes the complex impedance is defined strictly by a two-element series capacitive model :$$Z = R_s - j\frac{1}{\omega C_s}$$By equating the imaginary (reactive) components of the physical series $R-L-C$ network and the assumed series $R_s-C_s$ model, the operational relationship is defined as:$$-\frac{1}{\omega C_s} = \omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}$$To isolate the instrument-reported apparent series capacitance ($C_s$), both sides of the equation are multiplied by $-\omega$:$$\frac{1}{C_s} = \frac{1}{C} - \omega^2 L$$By finding a common denominator for the right-hand side of the expression:$$\frac{1}{C_s} = \frac{1 - \omega^2 L C}{C}$$Taking the reciprocal of both sides yields the exact formula utilized by the instrument to calculate and report $C_s$ :$$C_s = \frac{C}{1 - \omega^2 L C}$$Conversely, if the instrument is configured to extract series inductance ($L_s$), it assumes the complex impedance is represented strictly by a series inductive model :$$Z = R_s + j\omega L_s$$By equating the imaginary components of the physical series $R-L-C$ network and the assumed series $R_s-L_s$ model:$$\omega L_s = \omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}$$Dividing both sides by the angular frequency ($\omega$) yields the exact formula utilized by the instrument to calculate and report $L_s$ :$$L_s = L - \frac{1}{\omega^2 C}$$These two derived relationships demonstrate that the single-frequency spot parameters reported on the instrument display are mathematically dependent on the measurement frequency, the core capacitance, and the parasitic inductance.Empirical Validation and Theoretical Parameter Mapping of Film Capacitor SeriesTo validate these derivations, they are mapped directly to the raw dataset collected from the qualification of the TDK B32926, TDK B81123, and TDK B32922 capacitor series. At this frequency, the net imaginary component of the series network impedance drops to zero ($X = 0$), and the phase angle ($\theta$) is exactly $0^\circ$. The real part of the complex impedance is extracted from the vector magnitude and phase angle :$$\text{ESR} = |Z|\cos\theta$$For a highly conductive standard such as a thick copper plate, the true physical resistance $R$ is in the micro-ohm range, and the inductance is in the pico-henry range. As the test frequency scales, the inductive reactance ($X_L = \omega L$) begins to dominate, pushing the true phase angle ($\theta$) extremely close to $+90^\circ$.The mathematical sensitivity of the extracted resistance with respect to phase angle errors is determined by taking the partial derivative:$$\frac{\partial R}{\partial \theta} = \frac{\partial}{\partial \theta} \left(|Z|\cos\theta\right) = -|Z|\sin\theta$$Near $\theta \approx 90^\circ$, the term $\sin\theta \approx 1$. This indicates that the sensitivity of the resistance measurement to phase errors is maximized when the device is highly reactive. A minute phase measurement error ($\Delta \theta$) translates directly into a systematic offset in the calculated resistance:$$R_{\text{measured}} = |Z|\cos\left(\theta_{\text{true}} + \Delta \theta\right)$$Using trigonometric expansion, this relationship is expressed as:$$R_{\text{measured}} = |Z|\left(\cos\theta_{\text{true}}\cos\Delta\theta - \sin\theta_{\text{true}}\sin\Delta\theta\right)$$Because the true impedance vector is highly reactive ($\theta_{\text{true}} = 90^\circ - \delta$, where $\delta$ is the loss angle $\delta = R_{\text{true}}/|Z| \ll 1$), we substitute this into the expansion:$$\cos\theta_{\text{true}} = \cos\left(90^\circ - \delta\right) = \sin\delta \approx \delta$$$$\sin\theta_{\text{true}} = \sin\left(90^\circ - \delta\right) = \cos\delta \approx 1$$For a small phase measurement error ($\Delta\theta \ll 1$), the approximations $\cos\Delta\theta \approx 1$ and $\sin\Delta\theta \approx \Delta\theta$ are applied. This simplifies the measured resistance formula to:$$R_{\text{measured}} \approx |Z|\left(\delta - \Delta \theta\right) = |Z|\left(\frac{R_{\text{true}}}{|Z|} - \Delta\theta\right) = R_{\text{true}} - |Z|\Delta \theta$$This equation demonstrates that if the instrument's phase measurement error ($\Delta\theta$) is positive, the reported resistance is shifted downward. If the reactive magnitude of the component is large relative to its true resistance ($|Z|\Delta\theta > R_{\text{true}}$), the calculated resistance is forced into negative values.This phase sensitivity error is further compounded by contact resistance overcorrection during the baseline short calibration. The calibration algorithm compensates for the residual path resistance of the fixture using the relationship:$$R_{\text{corrected}} = R_{\text{raw}} - R_{\text{short\_cal}}$$During the baseline $\text{SHORT}$ calibration, the contacts are shorted using a calibration bar. However, if there is finite contact resistance or oxidation on the calibration bar, the instrument registers a non-zero residual resistance $R_{\text{short\_cal}}$ (e.g., $20\text{ m}\Omega$). When the actual measurement is executed on the highly polished, large-surface-area copper plate, the achieved contact resistance is lower than the contact resistance established during calibration ($R_{\text{raw}} < R_{\text{short\_cal}}$). Consequently, the subtraction overcorrects the path loss, forcing $R_{\text{corrected}}$ into negative values (specifically, $-300\text{ }\mu\Omega$ across the sub-megahertz range).At $20\,\text{MHz}$, the measured $\text{ESL}$ of the copper plate is $12.6\,\text{pH}$. The reactive magnitude is $|Z| \approx 2\pi f L = 2\pi \left(2 \times 10^7\right)\left(12.6 \times 10^{-12}\right) \approx 1.583\,\text{m}\Omega$. If a minute phase error of $\Delta\theta \approx +0.13^\circ$ ($2.27\,\text{mrad}$) occurs due to cable temperature variation or transimpedance stage propagation delay, the systematic offset is calculated as :$$\text{Offset} = -|Z|\Delta\theta \approx -\left(1.583 \times 10^{-3}\right) \times \left(2.27 \times 10^{-3}\right) \approx -3.59\,\mu\Omega$$While this phase offset is small, the combination of calibration standard overcorrection ($R_{\text{short\_cal}}$) and phase shift is the primary source of the negative resistance measurements.Instrumental and Algorithmic Limitations of the Keysight E4990ALimitations of Two-Element Approximation in Multi-Element NetworksThe primary operational limitation of the Keysight E4990A under standard LCR mode is its reliance on a two-element lumped parameter calculation. The instrument's internal firmware converts the measured complex voltage and current ratio into a simplified vector containing one real and one imaginary component ($R + jX$ or $G + jB$).This algorithmic reduction is based on the assumption that the component behaves as a single passive element with minor losses. However, physical metallized film capacitors are multi-element distributed networks. As the operating frequency increases, parameters such as the dielectric loss factor ($\tan\delta$), skin-effect-driven electrode resistance, parallel leakage paths, and the loop inductance of the terminal leads interact.When the E4990A attempts to map this multi-element reality to a two-element $R_s-C_s$ or $R_s-L_s$ configuration near or beyond the self-resonant frequency, the calculation is forced to utilize mathematical simplifications. The instrument does not dynamically adjust its model topology to account for the transitioning reactance. It executes the fixed algebraic extraction, outputting physically impossible values (such as negative capacitance and negative inductance). This creates a high risk of misinterpretation during component qualification.Auto-Balancing Bridge (ABB) Vector Impedance LimitsThe auto-balancing bridge ($\text{ABB}$) architecture of the E4990A utilizes a precision transimpedance amplifier configuration to maintain a virtual ground at the low-potential measurement node ($\text{L}_p$). This feedback loop balances the current flowing through the $\text{DUT}$ with the current flowing through an internal range reference resistor ($R_{\text{ref}}$). At the extreme boundaries of the vector impedance plane, the performance limits of this architecture become apparent:Low-Impedance Boundary ($|Z| \to 0\,\Omega$): When measuring the equivalent series resistance ($\text{ESR}$) of high-capacitance film capacitors or short-circuit validation plates, the impedance drop across the $\text{DUT}$ is extremely small. The voltage signal at the high-potential sensing node ($\text{H}_p$) drops toward the noise floor of the instrument's input amplifiers.Highly Reactive Phase Boundary ($\theta \to \pm 90^\circ$): For low-loss capacitors, the phase angle is close to $-90^\circ$. The real part of the vector, representing the component loss, is overshadowed by the dominant imaginary part. For example, at a quality factor $Q = \frac{1}{D} = 1000$, the resistive component represents only $0.1\%$ of the total impedance magnitude.Under these conditions, resolving the $\text{ESR}$ requires measuring the phase angle with micro-degree precision. Any phase measurement jitter, digitizer non-linearity, or thermal noise in the reference clock paths translates directly to phase noise.Because $\cos\left(90^\circ - \phi\right) \approx \phi$ (for small angles in radians), a phase measurement error of just $0.05^\circ$ ($872\,\mu\text{rad}$) in the transimpedance feedback loop will cause the resolved $\text{ESR}$ to deviate by up to $100\%$, often projecting the vector past $+90^\circ$ or $-90^\circ$, resulting in negative loss readings.If the voltage drive level is set too high (exceeding $500\,\text{mVrms}$) during low-impedance testing or SHORT compensation, the feedback amplifier of the transimpedance stage can saturate. The E4990A supports seven multi-parameter equivalent circuit models (Models A through G) to accommodate various component topologies. For standard, low-loss metallized film capacitors, Model A (a three-element series $R-L-C$ network) must be selected. If the component exhibits high dielectric leakage or is being characterized under high-temperature stress, Model B (a four-element parallel-series configuration incorporating a parallel leakage resistance $R_p$) should be utilized to isolate core dielectric losses from terminal losses.Once the model is selected, the fitting algorithm must be executed over the entire swept frequency range. The software performs regression fitting of the measured real ($R(\omega)$) and imaginary ($X(\omega)$) impedance components:$$Z_{\text{measured}}(\omega) \approx R_{\text{fit}} + j\left(\omega L_{\text{fit}} - \frac{1}{\omega C_{\text{fit}}}\right)$$This multi-frequency regression decouples the interactive reactive components. The resulting fitted values ($C_{\text{fit}}$, $L_{\text{fit}}$, $R_{\text{fit}}$) represent the physical construction of the capacitor. These values are frequency-independent and unaffected by resonance-induced sign-inversions or magnitude spikes.Advanced Calibration Verification RegimenTo ensure that phase errors are minimized and the instrument resolves micro-ohm and pico-henry impedances traceably, a calibration validation check must be performed prior to each qualification run:            Calibration Verification Flow & Tolerance Matrix

Step 1: Open Circuit Verification CheckAfter performing fixture compensation, measure the open-circuit impedance across the full frequency sweep. Run the equivalent circuit fitting routine on this open spectrum.The resulting fitted parameters must satisfy the following tolerance limits: the fitted residual capacitance ($C_{\text{fitted}}$) must be less than $15\,\text{fF}$, and the parallel resistance ($R_{\text{fitted}}$) must be greater than $10\,\text{k}\Omega$. If the registered open-circuit capacitance exceeds these limits, it indicates that the contact spacing was adjusted after calibration, requiring a recalibration.Step 2: Short Circuit Verification CheckMount the polished copper plate short-circuit standard in the clamping jaws. The negative equivalent series inductance ($\text{ESL}$) at lower frequencies, extreme capacitance inflation near resonance, and negative capacitance at high frequencies are the direct mathematical results of forcing a three-element series $R-L-C$ network into standard, single-frequency two-element calculations.Similarly, the negative $\text{ESR}$ values measured on the short-circuit copper plate standard are identified as systemic artifacts arising from phase calibration errors near the highly reactive $+90^\circ$ vector boundary and contact resistance overcorrection during short calibration.To eliminate these systematic measurement errors and ensure reliable component evaluation, the quality assurance and engineering divisions should implement the following recommendations:Decommission Single-Frequency Spot Inspections: Standardize on continuous multi-frequency sweeps for all capacitor qualifications. Single-frequency spot parameters should be utilized only for quick sorting and basic incoming inspections below $100\text{ kHz}$.Enforce Equivalent Circuit Analysis as the Primary Standard: Require the use of the E4990A's built-in equivalent circuit regression models (specifically Model A or Model B) for component characterization. The extracted, decoupled, frequency-independent values for core capacitance ($C$), loop inductance ($L$), and resistive loss ($R$) must be established as the formal metrics in qualification records.Establish a Formal Calibration and Verification Regimen: Require that every measurement sweep above $3\text{ MHz}$ utilize a comprehensive Open/Short/Load calibration protocol. Short calibration standards must have polished, low-resistance surfaces, and contact jaws must be regularly cleaned to prevent contact resistance variations.Implement Environmental Controls: Stabilize instrument thermal performance by enforcing a strict 90-minute warm-up period  and operating exclusively within temperature-controlled environments ($23\text{ }^\circ\text{C} \pm 5\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$) to minimize phase drift in the active bridge circuitry.
