Advanced Metrological Characterization of Nanohenry Inductance

This interactive engineering manual details the precise isolation of $1\,\text{nH}$ to $50\,\text{nH}$ Equivalent Series Inductance (ESL) and nominal capacitance in low-loss metallized film capacitors. As a Principal Metrology Engineer, you will explore six distinct extraction methodologies.

Critical Notice: Equivalent Circuit Analysis vs. Default Mode

Addressing the specific instrument behavior query: Obtaining negative capacitance ($-C$) and positive inductance ($+L$) in default $C_s-R_s$ or $L_s-R_s$ measurement modes at higher frequencies is the mathematically exact and expected behavior of Keysight/Keystone impedance analyzers. Above the Self-Resonant Frequency (SRF), the capacitor's parasitic ESL dominates the reactance, causing the net phase angle to flip from $-90^\circ$ to $+90^\circ$. The instrument algorithmically assumes a 2-element series model. Thus, a net inductive reactance is reported as a negative capacitance via $C_{app} = -1 / (\omega X_{net})$. Utilizing the instrument's Equivalent Circuit Analysis (ECA) mode fits the swept complex impedance curve to a multi-variable $L-C-R$ model, successfully resolving the true, static, positive $C$ and $L$ values simultaneously.

Baseline DUT Configuration

All solved numerical examples across the six methodologies utilize the following ultra-high-performance metallized film capacitor theoretical baseline:

  • Intrinsic Dielectric Capacitance ($C$): $2.919\,\mu\text{F}$
  • Intrinsic Equivalent Series Inductance ($L$): $20.90\,\text{nH}$
  • Intrinsic Equivalent Series Resistance ($R$): $15.01\,\text{m}\Omega$

Calculated Self-Resonant Frequency (SRF):

$$ f_{SRF} = \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{L C}} = \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{(20.90 \times 10^{-9})(2.919 \times 10^{-6})}} \approx 644.20\,\text{kHz} $$

1. Auto-Balanced Bridge (ABB) Method

Low-Mid Freq ($< 110\text{MHz}$)

The ABB architecture (e.g., Keysight E4990A) is the industry standard for sub-$100\,\text{MHz}$ characterization. It utilizes a virtual ground topology to null the current passing through the DUT, driving a precise feedback resistor to calculate complex impedance.

1. Theoretical Foundations & Mathematical Derivations

The instrument maintains a $0\,\text{V}$ potential at the $L_{cur}$ terminal via an I-V converter operational amplifier. The unknown complex impedance $Z_x$ is derived from the source voltage $V_1$ and the feedback voltage $V_2$ across the range resistor $R_r$.

$$ I_x = \frac{V_1}{Z_x}, \quad I_r = -\frac{V_2}{R_r} $$

By Kirchhoff's Current Law at the virtual ground, $I_x + I_r = 0$, yielding the governing extraction formula:

$$ Z_x = R_r \left( -\frac{V_1}{V_2} \right) = R_{ESR} + j\left( \omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C} \right) $$

2. Comprehensive Solved Numerical Example

Simulating the baseline DUT ($C=2.919\,\mu\text{F}$, $L=20.90\,\text{nH}$, $R=15.01\,\text{m}\Omega$) at two distinct test frequencies to demonstrate the phase inversion phenomenon.

Test Point A: $100\,\text{kHz}$ (Below SRF)

$$ \omega = 2\pi(10^5) = 6.28 \times 10^5\,\text{rad/s} $$
$$ X_C = -0.545\,\Omega, \quad X_L = 0.013\,\Omega $$
$$ Z_{total} = 0.015 - j0.532\,\Omega $$

Net capacitive. Apparent $C_{app} = 2.99\,\mu\text{F}$. Note the slight positive error due to uncompensated ESL.

Test Point B: $1\,\text{MHz}$ (Above SRF)

$$ \omega = 2\pi(10^6) = 6.28 \times 10^6\,\text{rad/s} $$
$$ X_C = -0.054\,\Omega, \quad X_L = 0.131\,\Omega $$
$$ Z_{total} = 0.015 + j0.077\,\Omega $$

Net inductive. Apparent $C_{app} = -2.07\,\mu\text{F}$ (Instrument displays negative C).

3. Practical Hardware Architecture & Bench Setup

Implementation requires a Four-Terminal Pair (4TP) configuration. The $H_{cur}$, $H_{pot}$, $L_{pot}$, and $L_{cur}$ coaxial lines must have their outer shields tied together directly at the fixture block to eliminate mutual inductance between source and return loops.

Vector Error Correction: Open/Short/Load compensation is mandatory. The 'Short' must be a zero-length, massive copper block bridging the exact terminal gap of the film capacitor to effectively zero out the residual $L_s$ of the test fixture contacts.

4. Analytical Accuracy & Uncertainty Budget

  • Phase Detector Resolution Limits: At $100\,\text{kHz}$, extracting $20\,\text{nH}$ ($X_L = 0.013\,\Omega$) from a $2.9\,\mu\text{F}$ ($X_C = 0.545\,\Omega$) background requires detecting a phase shift deviation of less than $0.001^\circ$ from $-90^\circ$. Standard ABB uncertainty limits absolute $L$ accuracy below $1\,\text{MHz}$ for high-$C$ values.
  • Cable Length Correction: Phase lag $\phi = \beta \ell$ down the 1m test leads introduces significant reflection error above $10\,\text{MHz}$ if the instrument's mathematical electrical length compensation is mismatched by even $1\,\text{mm}$.

5. Gauge R&R Framework

Primary Vulnerability: Operator Appraiser Variance ($\text{AV}$) dominates due to fixture insertion depth. Because $1\,\text{mm}$ of exposed lead wire adds approximately $1\,\text{nH}$ of parasitic inductance, variations in how operators clamp the DUT into the test fixture completely invalidate process capability ($\text{Cpk}$) limits for sub-$50\,\text{nH}$ components.

Mitigation: Utilize a pneumatic, depth-controlled, gold-plated Kelvin jaw fixture with a standardized $1.5\,\text{Nm}$ clamping force.

2. Voltage Surge Discharge Method

Time Domain

This method extracts ESL and ESR under actual high-current operating conditions ($>100\,\text{A}$) by discharging the capacitor into a dead short and capturing the resulting underdamped voltage or current ringing with a high-speed digitizer.

1. Theoretical Foundations & Mathematical Derivations

The DUT is charged to voltage $V_0$ and switched into a short circuit via a low-inductance solid-state switch. The system is modeled as a source-free series RLC circuit governed by the 2nd-order differential equation:

$$ L \frac{d^2 i(t)}{dt^2} + R \frac{di(t)}{dt} + \frac{1}{C} i(t) = 0 $$

Assuming underdamped conditions ($R < 2\sqrt{L/C}$), the discharge current is:

$$ i(t) = \frac{V_0}{L \omega_d} e^{-\alpha t} \sin(\omega_d t) $$

Where the damping factor $\alpha = \frac{R}{2L}$ and damped resonant frequency $\omega_d = \sqrt{\frac{1}{LC} - \alpha^2}$. ESL is extrapolated by measuring the ringing period $T_d = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_d}$ and logarithmic decrement.

$$ L_{ESL} = \frac{1}{C \left( \left(\frac{2\pi}{T_d}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\ln(I_1/I_2)}{T_d}\right)^2 \right)} - L_{fixture} $$

2. Comprehensive Solved Numerical Example

Transient Parameters

Baseline DUT charged to $V_0 = 1000\,\text{V}$.

$$ \alpha = \frac{15.01 \times 10^{-3}}{2 \times 20.9 \times 10^{-9}} = 3.59 \times 10^5\,\text{s}^{-1} $$
$$ \omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = 4.048 \times 10^6\,\text{rad/s} $$
$$ \omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \alpha^2} \approx 4.032 \times 10^6\,\text{rad/s} $$

Ringing Frequency $f_d \approx 641.7\,\text{kHz}$. Peak current $I_{peak} \approx 11.5\,\text{kA}$ at $t = 380\,\text{ns}$.

3. Practical Hardware Architecture & Bench Setup

Requires a high-voltage DC supply, an ultra-fast thyristor or spark gap trigger, a Pearson current monitor (or coaxial current shunt), and an oscilloscope with $\ge 1\,\text{GS/s}$ sampling rate. The switch geometry is critical; a custom low-inductance planar busbar ($L_{fixture} < 5\,\text{nH}$) is required to ensure the fixture inductance does not mask the DUT's $20\,\text{nH}$ ESL.

4. Analytical Accuracy & Uncertainty Budget

Primary Error Vector: $L_{fixture}$ de-embedding. The fixture inductance is typically characterized by firing the surge loop with a solid copper slug replacing the DUT. If the high-current magnetic skin effect causes $L_{fixture}$ to vary dynamically during the ringing cycle, the rigid $L_{ESL}$ extrapolation fails. Accuracy limits are practically $\pm 2\,\text{nH}$.

3. VNA 2-Port Shunt-Thru Method

RF / High Freq ($> 1\text{MHz}$)

The standard impedance reflection ($S_{11}$) method fails for sub-ohm impedances. The 2-Port Shunt-Thru method mounts the capacitor in parallel with the VNA transmission line, providing exceptional sensitivity for ultra-low impedance magnitudes in the milliohm regime, perfect for large $C$ and tiny $L$.

1. Theoretical Foundations & Mathematical Derivations

The DUT is shunted across a continuous $50\,\Omega$ transmission line connecting Port 1 to Port 2. The $S_{21}$ forward transmission coefficient represents the voltage divided between the source impedance, the DUT impedance, and the load impedance.

$$ S_{21} = \frac{2 Z_x}{2 Z_x + Z_0} $$

Isolating the complex impedance $Z_x$ of the capacitor yields:

$$ Z_x = \frac{Z_0}{2} \left( \frac{S_{21}}{1 - S_{21}} \right) = 25 \left( \frac{S_{21}}{1 - S_{21}} \right) $$

2. Comprehensive Solved Numerical Example

Test Point: $10\,\text{MHz}$ Evaluation

Baseline DUT at a frequency deep into the inductive region.

$$ X_L = 2\pi(10^7)(20.9 \times 10^{-9}) = 1.313\,\Omega $$
$$ X_C = -\frac{1}{2\pi(10^7)(2.919 \times 10^{-6})} = -0.005\,\Omega $$
$$ Z_x = 0.01501 + j1.308\,\Omega $$

Calculate expected $S_{21}$:

$$ S_{21} = \frac{2(0.01501 + j1.308)}{2(0.01501 + j1.308) + 50} \approx 0.0015 + j0.052 $$

Magnitude $|S_{21}| \approx 0.052$ (approx $-25.7\,\text{dB}$). Phase $\approx 88.3^\circ$.

3. Practical Hardware Architecture & Bench Setup

Requires a wideband VNA (e.g., Keysight E5061B), two high-quality $50\,\Omega$ phase-stable coaxial cables, and a specialized coplanar waveguide PCB fixture. The capacitor must be soldered or pressure-mounted precisely at the midpoint of the through-line.

Vector Error Correction: Full 2-Port SOLT (Short-Open-Load-Thru) calibration at the coax ends, followed by port extension, or Advanced Fixture De-embedding (using Touchstone $S2P$ files of the bare fixture) to shift the reference plane to the PCB pad boundaries.

4. Analytical Accuracy & Uncertainty Budget

Mutual Coupling Error: The primary failure mode of this method is the parasitic mutual inductance ($M$) between the ground return paths of Port 1 and Port 2. A braided shield ground return introduces a transfer impedance that limits the minimum measurable ESR and creates artificial dips in the ESL extraction. Uncertainty rises sharply below $1\,\text{m}\Omega$ or $1\,\text{nH}$. Employing a solid copper block ground plane and common-mode chokes on the coaxial lines mitigates this ground-loop error.

4. Radio Frequency Current-Voltage (RF I-V) Method

Bridging the gap between ABB and VNA reflection architectures, the RF I-V method utilizes a high-frequency probe head containing integrated broad-band voltage and current transformers. It extends the direct $Z = V/I$ paradigm up to $3\,\text{GHz}$.

1. Theoretical Foundations & Mathematical Derivations

The probe head injects an RF signal. A directional voltage transformer detects $V_x$ across the DUT, while a toroidal current transformer detects $I_x$.

$$ Z_x = \frac{V_x}{I_x} \cdot e^{j(\theta_V - \theta_I)} $$

The instrument down-converts the RF signals to IF via precision mixers, digitizes them, and processes the vector ratio. Because the transformers are matched to a characteristic $50\,\Omega$ environment, standing waves within the probe head are minimized.

2. Practical Hardware Architecture & Bench Setup

Utilizes instruments like the Keysight E4991B with a $7\,\text{mm}$ coaxial test fixture interface. High-precision $7\,\text{mm}$ APC connectors interface the base unit to a bottom-electrode SMD test fixture (e.g., 16197A). Calibration requires a highly specialized Open/Short/Load set physically matching the footprint of the film capacitor, followed by electrical length compensation.

3. Gauge R&R Framework

Reproducibility Vulnerability: The RF I-V method is hyper-sensitive to the electromagnetic environment immediately surrounding the DUT. Operator hands moving near the unshielded fixture, or minute variations in the rotational alignment of the capacitor on the $7\,\text{mm}$ coaxial electrodes, alter the fringing capacitance ($C_f$) and stray mutual inductance ($L_m$). Gauge R&R relies entirely on strict, automated pick-and-place fixturing inside an RF-shielded dark box.

5. Resonant Cavity / Perturbation Method

Scalar Specific

While less common for lumped macroscopic film capacitors, perturbation techniques measure the macroscopic effective permittivity and permeability, providing a scalar, ultra-high $Q$ extraction method. The capacitor alters the electromagnetic boundary conditions of a high-$Q$ cavity.

1. Theoretical Foundations & Mathematical Derivations

The baseline resonant frequency $f_0$ and unloaded quality factor $Q_0$ of a $TM_{010}$ cylindrical cavity are measured via a scalar or vector network analyzer. The DUT is inserted into the maximum electric or magnetic field region. The shift in resonance $\Delta f$ and degradation in quality factor $\Delta Q$ correlate directly to the reactive and resistive properties of the DUT.

$$ \frac{f_0 - f_s}{f_0} \approx \frac{1}{2} \iiint_{V_s} (\epsilon_r - 1) |E_0|^2 dV $$

For lumped parameter extraction, the cavity mode is modeled as a lumped $L_c - C_c$ tank, where the DUT acts as a massive detuning element. The extreme detuning caused by a $2.9\,\mu\text{F}$ capacitor makes standard perturbation theory break down; thus, custom cavity scaling or substitution techniques are required.

2. Analytical Accuracy & Uncertainty Budget

This method suffers from extreme geometry dependence. The exact position, orientation, and volumetric displacement of the metallized film capacitor inside the cavity fundamentally dictate the frequency shift. Therefore, while $Q$ measurements are unparalleled for extremely low-loss dielectrics, absolute accuracy for lumped $L_{ESL}$ extraction is poor ($\pm 20\%$) due to field-fringing complexities.

6. Time Domain Reflectometry (TDR) Method

TDR fires an ultra-fast voltage step into the DUT interface. By monitoring the reflected wave, the spatial distribution of impedance—including the lumped ESL spike—is mapped temporally.

1. Theoretical Foundations & Mathematical Derivations

A fast-rise-time step $V_{in}(t)$ (e.g., $t_r = 35\,\text{ps}$) is launched down a $Z_0 = 50\,\Omega$ transmission line. The instantaneous reflection coefficient $\rho(t)$ is recorded:

$$ \rho(t) = \frac{V_{ref}(t)}{V_{in}(t)} = \frac{Z_L(t) - Z_0}{Z_L(t) + Z_0} $$

A series $L-C$ network produces a distinct signature. Initially, the capacitor acts as a short, but the inductor opposes the step, causing a positive voltage spike. The area under this reflected inductive spike is directly proportional to the ESL.

$$ L_{ESL} \approx \frac{Z_0}{2} \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \rho(t) \, dt \quad \text{(for small } L \text{)} $$

2. Comprehensive Solved Numerical Example

TDR Integral Calculation

Baseline DUT ($L=20.9\,\text{nH}$). Assuming an ideal step excitation and isolating the initial inductive response before the capacitor begins to charge significantly.

$$ \int \rho(t) \, dt \approx \frac{2 \times 20.9 \times 10^{-9}}{50} = 0.836\,\text{ns} $$

The oscilloscope will display a positive reflection spike with an area of $0.836\,\text{ns}$ (e.g., reaching $\rho = +0.8$ with a width of $\sim 1\,\text{ns}$).

3. Long-Term Reliability & Stability Analysis

TDR requires high-bandwidth sampling oscilloscopes. The primary degradation mode is the physical wear on the microwave sampler diodes and the $3.5\,\text{mm}$ or SMA connectors used to launch the step. Thermal drift of the trigger delay logic creates baseline wander, requiring frequent recalibration (e.g., every 30 minutes) using standard air-lines to maintain picosecond temporal accuracy.
